What’s in the night sky? (March edition)

What’s in the night sky?,

For this month, our winter constellations like Orion, Taurus, Auriga and Gemini are setting slowly after sunset so this is the last month to get a good view of these constellations. Can’t forget either that Mars will be near Taurus throughout the month and move towards Gemini by the end of the month so also make sure to check the red planet out while you can! 

Although the winter constellations will be lowering throughout this month, constellations like the Lynx and Cancer will be rising in the night sky. They are quite faint in the sky so the best time to view them will be on the 21st of March where we will have a new moon! You can also try to spot M44 The Beehive Cluster in the constellation of Cancer! 

This day is also an opportunity to observe the zodiacal light! This appears as a diffuse glow emanating from the horizon and it is caused by interplanetary dust reflecting sunlight into the night sky. In the Northern hemisphere it is best seen in mid-latitudes towards the west after sunset. Near the equator it can be seen all year around and in the Southern hemisphere it is best viewed facing east in the hours before dawn. So get out there and just look up! 

Photo and information taken from the video What's in the Night Sky March 2023 🌌 Ramadan Moon | Venus-Jupiter Conjunction by Alyn Wallace. Click the link to find out more!

 

Photo gallery

Photo gallery,

A set of new photos have been released from the Curiosity rover on Mars, showing the Sun’s rays streaming through clouds on Mars! Mars has a thin atmosphere that is very different to ours, yet we still see similar effects like this on other planets in our Solar System! In a similar vein, another photo was released showing high level clouds in Mars’ atmosphere being lit up by the Sun.

These photos were taken in January but have only recently been released by NASA. According to atmospheric scientists, the colour changes seen in the cloud represent different sized particles that make up the cloud. These colours come from different scattering effects.

To read more click the following link: https://www.nasa.gov/feature/jpl/nasa-s-curiosity-views-first-sun-rays-on-mars 

 

Space News

Space News,

This week a Japanese astronomer captured a meteorite crashing into the surface of the Moon! The meteorite was travelling at around 13.1km/s towards the surface, and added a new crater to the surface of the Moon. Current estimates put the size of the crater at around 12m, large enough to be imaged by the LRO (Lunar Reconnaissance Orbiter) at some point in the future.

Click here to find out more! https://www.space.com/meteorite-impacts-moon-february-2023-video
