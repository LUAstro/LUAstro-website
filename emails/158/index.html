Jodrell Bank Trip

Jodrell Bank Trip,

Over the weekend we had our first ever group trip to Jodrell Bank! A group of eight travelled to the Cheshire countryside and spent several hours touring the exhibits and watching the short films they had to offer (and getting lost in the expansive grounds at one point). Not only did we manage to experience the brand new First Light pavilion, featuring the story of the 250ft telescope, but we also managed to catch the final day of the Apollo Remastered exhibit - featuring some previously undeveloped images of man on the moon. It was a thoroughly good day out, which everyone enjoyed just as thoroughly, and we would like to extend our thanks, once again, for everyone who turned up.Stay in the loop as we can’t wait to do more of these kinds of trips in the future!

 

 

What’s in the night sky? (March edition)

What’s in the night sky?,

As the end of the month approaches, we wanted to remind you of the things you can still observe in the days that we have left in March!

The 24th March is the best day to watch Uranus, Venus and the Moon close together! We’d say the best time to see it would be between 7pm and 9pm. 

Again, this is the last good month to look at Orion, Auriga, Taurus, Gemini and Canis Major to name a few of the winter constellations. They will be setting shortly after sunset so it’s the last good view of them! 

It is also the return of the Milky Way core! Face East/Southeast in the mornings of the latter half of the month, can see the milky way core rising! It is a good occasion for a milky way arch panorama facing east. 

So get out there and look up!

 

Photo gallery

Photo gallery,

This week we got a new JWST image!

 

This is a kind of star called a Wolf-Rayet star. These stars show incredibly high surface temperatures and a defining lack of Hydrogen in its spectra. Some astrophysicists believe that these are stars that are nearing the ends of their main sequence lives. Wolf-Rayet stars are unique in that they emit pulses of stellar winds in the form of dust and gas. These pulses (seen around the star) can be used to work out the mass history of the initial star!

It turns out as well we have already taken a photo of a Wolf-Rayet star ourselves! This is a photo of the Crescent nebula. 

 

At the center of this nebula is a Wolf-Rayet star! It has the very imaginative name of WR 136. Currently, our estimations put the brightness of this star at around 600,000 times brighter than our Sun! It is also 21 times as massive and 5.1 times larger than our Sun. We talked about high surface temperatures, well this star’s surface is burning at ~70,000K (for context, our Sun has a surface temp. of 5800K)!

 

Space News

Space News,

JWST has observed a new exoplanet with a very interesting spectrum! VHS 1256 b is a world orbiting around VHS 1256 41.4 lightyears away from us and has a mass of 19 times the mass of Jupiter. Recent observations from JWST show that the planet contains silicates in its outer atmosphere! It is being likened to Tatooine originally seen in Star Wars: A New Hope from 1977, since it contains sand and orbits a binary star system.

Another interesting part to this discovery is how it was made. For the first time, these measurements were done via directly imaging the planet itself. Usually, these measurements are made by analysing the light that passes through the atmosphere of a planet from its parent star. This shows (again!) how powerful JWST truly is for exoplanet analysis!

 

The first map of water concentration on the moon was released recently, using data from NASA’s aeroplane-mounted observatory SOFIA. Observing part of the moon at an infrared wavelength of 6 micrometres made sure the data wasn’t interfered with by the similar molecule hydroxyl, which was a problem even for probes orbiting the moon in search of water. In the map water can be seen to concentrate in darker regions as expected, but it is still unknown where the water comes from, one possibility being evaporation from our atmosphere!
