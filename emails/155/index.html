Jodrell Bank Trip

Jodrell Bank Trip,

On March 19th at 10am, we are going to the Jodrell Bank, a UNESCO world heritage radio telescope just outside of Manchester. It is the world's third largest steerable dish radio telescope, instrumental in the discovery of cosmic rays, meteoroids, quasars, pulsars, masers and gravitational lenses, and was heavily involved with the tracking of space probes at the start of the Space Age.

The day will involve driving there for 11:30am, to see the exhibits and the telescope in all its glory. Exhibits include the new First Light Discovery centre, highlighting the history of the telescope, several exhibits on cosmology, an expansive grounds with a scale solar system and a clockwork orrery (and, of course, two cafes). We will be home in Lancaster by 6 at the latest, but if there is a consensus to go before that, we can be back earlier. 

If you are interested, fill out the form here (total cost is a tenner)! If you have a car and are willing to drive people, let us know.


 

Photo gallery

Photo gallery,

This week saw a close approach of Venus and Jupiter in the evening sky! In this image from Lee Duigon, Jupiter’s largest moons are very clearly visible alongside the planet itself, with Venus on the right appearing bigger on the sky. This is an optical illusion, stemming from the much brighter Venus out-shining Jupiter due to Venus’ thicker atmosphere reflecting more light towards us.

Space News

Space News,

After launching from Kennedy Space Centre on a SpaceX Falcon 9, Crew-6 is now on the ISS. The 6 meaning this was the 6th SpaceX launch of an ISS team on a Crew Dragon spacecraft. Two NASA astronauts, a Russian cosmonaut and a UAE astronaut make up the group. A docking sensor required a software override to be sent from the ground to connect the last hook to the ISS, after an hour waiting in position.

Crew-5, another group of 4 currently on the ISS, are planned to leave it later this month.
