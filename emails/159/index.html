AGM & Hustings

AGM & Hustings,

Photo gallery

Photo gallery,

The first image is of a supernova remnant (SNR), in the direction of the Cassiopeia constellation. The shell is currently expanding by 5000 km every second, causing it to lose its title as the brightest measured object outside the solar system from 1980. In this image we see it around 300 years after the supernova explosion, although in reality 11,000 years have passed, since it is that many lightyears away. This makes it visibly the youngest known SNR in our galaxy, and useful to astronomers to learn about supernovae.

 

JWST has already taken pictures of Mars, Jupiter and Neptune, and with this first Uranus image from the telescope, the only planet left is Saturn! (The other planets are too close to the sun, which would heat up the telescope if it pointed at them, ruining the images) Uranus’s rings are visible, as well as its larger moons, with galaxies in the background. The brighter region of the planet is a ‘polar cap’, which unlike ours faces the sun, as Uranus’ rotates on its side.

Space News

Space News,

The Artemis II crew has been selected, three NASA astronauts and one from the Canadian space agency. Current plans are for them to make a “close” approach to the moon (10,000 km), test the life support, manoeuvring, optical communication and other systems. There will be ten days between launch and splashdown, with the four astronauts being the first people to be further from earth than the space stations since the end of Apollo in 1972.
 

ESA's "JUICE" (Jupiter Icy Moons Explorer) just launched today, which will arrive at Jupiter in 2031, after performing many flybys to gain the speed to get there. The satellite will gather data on Jupiter and three of its moons, Europa, Ganymede and Callisto, all thought to contain water ice. The mission focuses on Ganymede, with the satellite orbiting the moon for a year, learning about its composition and magnetosphere.
