Space News 

Space News ,

In the past few days, the James Webb Space Telescope accidentally found an asteroid, it being the smallest object to date that it has ever detected. 

The asteroid is about 100-200m across making it about the size of the Rome Colosseum making it the smallest object ever detected by JWST. 

The data for this discovery is from the calibration of the Mid-InfraRed Instrument on the telescope in which the asteroid was caught accidentally. You can check it out in this video!

You can read more in "The James Webb Space Telescope just found an asteroid by total accident, its smallest object yet" written on space.com website and here as well "Webb detects extremely small main-belt asteroid" on the ESA website.

Photo gallery 

Photo gallery ,

This week we have two photos from our exec members taken during Saturday's observing session. 

The first photo is yet another stunning picture of the C/2022E3 ZTF comet by our president Orlando taken with our small refractor. Don’t forget to observe it while you still can!

The second photo is a gorgeous photo of M87 and the Markarian's chain by our former president Jacob. When zooming in the picture you can see M87 in the centre and you will find a few other fuzzy blobs which are other galaxies in the Markarian’s chain! This photo was taken with Jacob’s own camera, a Canon 2000D with a 300mm lens.
